Health records: charts, coding and requests

For hospitals that still run paper charts alongside their systems. Every chart movement is recorded with a tap, so tomorrow's clinic charts are found today. Coders see uncoded discharges by age. The records manager sees charts out too long, the coding backlog and record requests against their due dates. Patients appear only as anonymous record codes; nothing clinical is shown. The guide suggests; a person decides.

What happened

    Guide

    The guide reads chart locations, tomorrow's pull list, the coding queue and request due dates, and suggests where to look, with its reasons. In this preview it uses clear, checkable rules that run in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere. A suggestion only becomes a proposal; the records manager confirms it. It never reads clinical content and never suggests codes.

    Everything here is invented: Example General Hospital, its wards, clinics, shelf bays, record codes, dates and times. Patients appear only as anonymous record codes, and no clinical content is shown. Periods and due dates are example settings, not standards; the legal period for answering record requests depends on where you are. It measures charts, queues and requests, never individual clerks or coders, and it does not code anything or give coding advice. Coming next (Feb 2027): duplicate record review for a person to confirm or reject, an access audit trail, breach reports that start a response clock, and retention schedules for approval.
