Infection prevention operations: isolation rooms, audits and closures

Wards see their isolation rooms live and ask for one in a few taps. Hand hygiene and cleaning audits go in on a phone and count the same day. The infection prevention and control (IPC) team runs one board for rooms, requests, audits and a ward in closure mode. The guide points out matches and gaps; a person decides.

What happened today

    Guide

    The guide reads room status, waiting requests, audit dates and closures, and suggests where to look, with its reasons. In this preview it uses clear, checkable rules that run in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere. A suggestion only becomes a proposal. The IPC team decides.

    Everything here is invented: Example General Hospital, its wards, rooms, codes and figures. It handles rooms, requests, audits and closures only. It does not diagnose infection, decide who needs isolation, or advise on treatment; clinicians decide. Audits count observations, never named staff. Coming next (Feb 2027): audit results by area and month with control limits and linked actions, a monthly audit round planner, enhanced and terminal clean requests sent straight to the cleaning team, and device-days counted per unit as the denominator for device infection rates.
