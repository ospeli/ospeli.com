IT, digital and cyber

Any staff member reports an IT problem from a phone in a few taps and sees which systems are up, slow or down. The service desk works one queue and switches a system into downtime, which tells the affected departments. IT and data protection leads see open tickets, downtime, leaver accounts and device updates as counts. The guide spots patterns; a person decides.

What happened

    Guide

    The guide reads open tickets, system status, restore tests and access counts, and suggests where to look, with its reasons. In this preview it uses clear, checkable rules that run in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere. A suggestion only becomes a proposal; a named role confirms it. Ospeli never disables accounts or changes other systems.

    Everything here is invented: Example General Hospital, its areas, systems, tickets and counts. It measures systems, tickets and access, never individual staff, and it does not monitor anyone's computer use. It is not antivirus or network monitoring. Coming next (Feb 2027): report a fault by scanning a QR label on a device, downtime mode with printable paper forms and a catch-up entry list, backup restore test tracking, and playbooks for downtime drills, quarterly access reviews and the first hour of a cyber incident.
