Pharmacy operations: requests, dispensing and stock

Wards ask for top-ups, non-stock items and discharge medicines from a phone, and see where each request is. The dispensary works one queue by type and marks each stage. The pharmacy manager sees turnaround, the queue against staff on shift, stock-outs and items near expiry. The guide points out where to look; a person decides.

What happened

    Guide

    The guide reads the queue, stock levels, expiry dates and the bed board's count of patients waiting for a bed, and suggests where to look, with its reasons. In this preview it uses clear, checkable rules that run in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere. A suggestion only becomes a proposal. The pharmacist or pharmacy manager decides.

    Everything here is invented: Example General Hospital, its wards, stock lines, patient codes, requests and times. Stock lines are plain labels, not real products. It tracks requests, stages and stock; it does not choose medicines, doses or substitutions, check prescriptions clinically, or rank pharmacy staff. Coming next (Jan 2027): stock counts by location on a phone, top-up levels per ward, reorder drafts from use and lead time, and the live link from planned discharges on the bed board.
