Diagnostics: lab samples and scans, request to report

Every lab sample and scan carries a time at each handoff: requested, collected, received in the lab, reported. Wards see where theirs is without phoning. Lab reception and imaging booking work from live lists. The manager sees turnaround by test and step, rejections and equipment downtime. Ospeli tracks the journey only; it never shows or reads results.

What happened

    Guide

    The guide reads the sample and scan times, rejections and equipment status, and suggests where to look, with its reasons. In this preview it uses clear, checkable rules that run in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere. A suggestion only becomes a proposal. A named role decides.

    Everything here is invented: Example General Hospital, its wards, patient codes, samples, scans and times. Patients appear only as anonymous codes. Ospeli tracks requests, samples and turnaround; it does not show or interpret results, choose tests or put patients in clinical order, and it never times individual scientists or radiographers. Turnaround times and other limits are example settings, not a standard. CT is computed tomography; MRI is magnetic resonance imaging. Coming next (Jan 2027): critical-result phone-call times, missing-sample alerts from porter jobs, analyser queues, and diagnostic waits that hold discharges, theatres or the emergency department.
