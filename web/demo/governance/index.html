Governance: policies, accreditation and committees

Every policy has an owner, a version and a review date. Staff find the current one from a phone and tick when they have read a change. Standards link to their evidence, so readiness shows all year. Committee decisions become one action list. The guide points to what needs attention; a person decides.

What happened

    Guide

    The guide reads review dates, drafts, read-and-understood counts and the self-assessment, and suggests where to look, with its reasons. In this preview it uses clear, checkable rules that run in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere. A suggestion only becomes a proposal. The governance lead decides.

    Everything here is invented: Example General Hospital, its areas, documents, standards, meetings and counts. Document titles and summaries are made-up examples, not guidance to follow, and the standard chapters are generic, not any accreditor's text. Ospeli does not write or approve clinical guideline content; people approve every document. It counts acknowledgements by area and role, never by named person.
