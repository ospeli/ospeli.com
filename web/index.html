The problem

Too much of hospital operations still runs on walking, phoning and paper.

Beds, broken equipment, emergency department queues and handoffs are tracked on paper, whiteboards and phone calls, so nobody can see the whole hospital or prove what an improvement changed. Ospeli builds that record first.

How it works

Capture once. Every answer builds on it.

The model is not the hard part. The data, the hospital's structure and the routines that use them are.

  1. Capture

    One tap on a shared tablet or phone. Works offline.

    Staff record what happens with one tap on a shared tablet or phone: a bed freed, a fault reported, a patient triaged. It works offline; sync between devices is in development. Timestamps are a by-product, not extra work.

  2. Model

    Wards, beds, roles and definitions, set up once.

    Departments, wards, beds, assets, roles and metric definitions are configured once per hospital, without code. Every report uses the same definitions every day.

  3. Playbooks

    Routines turn the day's events into reports.

    Ready-made routines turn the day's events into the outputs operations teams already produce: flow reports, redesign options, structure reviews.

  4. Decide

    Ospeli suggests; a named person decides.

    Ospeli suggests; a named person decides. Every suggestion is logged with its inputs and the human action taken.

Playbooks

The work your operations team does every week, built in.

Each playbook has a fixed input, a fixed analysis and a ready-to-use output. No starting from a blank chat.

Why not a general AI chat?

A chat assistant starts every conversation empty.

Most of the effort in using general AI for hospital operations goes into explaining the hospital. Ospeli already knows it.

  • Knows only what someone pastes in
  • Needs the wards, roles and definitions explained each time
  • Gives a different answer to the same question on different days
  • Sends whatever you paste to an outside service

Ospeli

  • Holds today's data, captured on the ward as work happens

  • Runs the same playbook the same way, so results compare week to week
  • Designed to run on your own server; no patient data leaves the hospital
How it is designed

Designed for unreliable networks, shared tablets and tight budgets.

Offline-first

A ward keeps working through a network outage. Sync between devices is in development.

Runs on your premises

Designed to run on one small local server per hospital, with cloud hosting optional and in-country where the law requires. Today's demo runs in your browser.

Beside your EHR

Designed to sit alongside your record system, never replace it. FHIR R4 export is planned, with HL7 v2 feeds after that.

Export anything

Export everything as an open JSON file today. CSV and FHIR export are planned. Your data stays yours.

Commodity hardware

Mid-range Android tablets, a mini PC and a UPS. No specialist kit required.

Open core

The core will be open source from March 2027, so your own IT team, or a supplier you choose, can install and inspect it.

Governance

Safe to buy, safe to run.

Ospeli is built to the questions a hospital board, governance lead and data protection officer ask first.

People decide, Ospeli suggests

No allocation, report or clinical draft is issued without a named person's action.

Patient data stays in the hospital

Nothing identifiable leaves the site unless the hospital approves that specific flow in writing.

Measures flows, never people

Ospeli measures beds, queues and handoffs by role. It never times or ranks a named individual.

Full audit trail

Every change, and every agent read, is recorded in an append-only trail. Tamper-evidence that survives a device reset is in development.

Synthetic data until you approve

Demonstrations and testing use a fictional hospital. Real data is used only after formal written approval.

An off switch for every AI feature

Each assistive feature can be turned off per hospital and per ward.

Start with one to three wards and the bed desk.

First pilots start in March 2027. A pilot runs alongside your paper process; paper is retired on a ward only if its staff choose to.

    Measure today's baselines on paper. Agree ward and bed names. Check clocks. Install tablets.

  2. Weeks 3–6One ward and the bed desk live

    One ward and the allocation desk go live, alongside paper.

  3. Weeks 7–12Two more wards; results measured

    Two more wards join. Results are measured against the week-one baseline.
