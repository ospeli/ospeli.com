Supply chain and stores

Shows what stock each area has, what is running out, and what's ordered.

Open the app (stores and supplies)

Built on the shared core today. The demo uses fictional data.

Common patterns we design forWhere there is no system: Wards often find out an item is gone when the box is empty. Some areas hold extra while others run short. Requests often go on paper, and it is hard to see where an order is in the purchasing process.
Where there is a record or tracking system: An inventory or purchasing system holds central stock, but ward-level stock and the status of a request are often invisible to the people waiting.
Ospeli works alongside existing systems.
With OspeliEach area has fill-to levels and quick counts on a phone. Low stock raises a request; orders show each step from request to delivery. Stock-outs are logged with their cause.

Who uses it

Charge nurseCounts ward stock on a phone, requests top-up, sees when it arrives.
Stores assistantIssues requests, receives deliveries, scans or counts items in and out.
Supply chain managerSets fill-to levels, tracks orders through purchasing, reviews stock-outs and slow stock.
Operations managerSees stock-outs that stopped care and orders stuck awaiting approval.

Key screens

  • Ward stock count (phone): Items for this area, current count against fill-to level, one-tap request.
  • Requests and issues: Open requests by area, status, items short, and time waiting.
  • Order tracker: Each order from request to approval, order, delivery and receipt, with days at each step.
  • Stock-out log: Items out of stock by area, duration, cause and impact on care.

What it records

  • Stock count by area: shows real ward stock, not just stores.
  • Request raised and issued: measures fill rate and wait.
  • Order approved, placed, delivered, received: shows where orders stall.
  • Stock-out start and end with cause: finds repeat gaps.
  • Donation received with item details: brings donations into stock.
  • Expiry and write-off: shows waste and over-ordering.

What the guide suggests

  • When an item's use runs ahead of its fill-to level, suggests a new level, showing recent usage.
  • When an order has waited at one approval step longer than usual, suggests a reminder to the approving role.
  • When one area holds far more than its level while another is short, suggests a transfer between areas.

Every suggestion shows its reason. A person decides. The guide has an off switch.

Playbooks

  • Fill-to level reset: review usage by area, output new levels.
  • Stock-out review: trace each stock-out's cause, output fixes per step.

Standards and methods it is designed around

Ospeli is not certified against any of these. Each line says how the area relates to the standard or method.

Standards and references

  • GS1 healthcare standards: Item records are designed to hold GS1 identifiers where your suppliers provide them.
  • ISO 9001: Request, order and stock-out records are designed to support a quality management system that your organisation runs.
  • JCI Governance, Leadership, and Direction (GLD): Purchasing steps and approval records are designed to be usable as evidence when your organisation prepares for this chapter.
