Common patterns we design forWhere there is no system: Audits are often tallied by hand and reported late. It is often unclear which isolation rooms are free. In an outbreak, closed bays, cleaning and staff lists are often tracked on whiteboards and phone calls.
Where there is a record or tracking system: A surveillance system holds infection data, but isolation rooms, closures and cleaning are often coordinated outside it.
Ospeli works alongside existing systems.
With OspeliAudits are entered on a phone and roll up the same day. Isolation rooms show live status. An outbreak gets one board for closed areas, deep cleans, supplies and actions.

Who uses it

IPC nurseRuns audits by phone, manages the outbreak board, sets area restrictions.
Charge nurseRequests an isolation room and sees area restrictions for the unit.
Patient flow coordinatorSees isolation rooms free and closed bays when placing patients.
Cleaning supervisorReceives enhanced and terminal clean requests, marks them done.
IPC committee chairReviews audit trends, outbreak timelines and open actions.

Key screens

  • Audit (phone): Hand hygiene or environment audit form, offline, with instant feedback score for the area.
  • Isolation room board: Each isolation room: free, occupied, cleaning, out of service, with time in state.
  • Outbreak board: Closed areas, restrictions, cleans due, supplies, actions and daily status.
  • Audit results: Compliance by area and month on SPC charts, with linked actions.

What it records

  • Hand hygiene opportunity observed and outcome: feeds compliance rate.
  • Isolation room requested and assigned: measures wait for isolation.
  • Area closed and reopened: outbreak duration and bed-days lost.
  • Enhanced or terminal clean requested and completed: links to cleaning.
  • Environment audit item failed: creates a work order or action.
  • Device-days counted per unit: a count passed to your infection surveillance system.

What the guide suggests

  • When all isolation rooms are full and two requests wait, suggests the flow coordinator review room use.
  • When an area's audit score falls below its SPC limit, suggests a repeat audit and feedback session.
  • When an area is reopened, drafts a checklist of cleans, restocking and notices to clear.

Every suggestion shows its reason. A person decides. The guide has an off switch.

Playbooks

  • Outbreak response: area closure, clean schedule, supplies, daily status note.
  • Monthly audit round: schedule by area, results pack and actions.

Standards and references

  • WHO Core Components for IPC Programmes: Used as a reference for the audit and monitoring records.
  • JCI Hospital Standards (prevention and control of infections): Audit, isolation room and area-closure records are designed to be usable as evidence when your organisation prepares for these standards.
